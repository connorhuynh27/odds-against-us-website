The Issue

Youth gambling in a 24/7 world

Gambling used to require a trip to the casino. Now it's a notification away. It's open 24/7, always on the phone in your pocket, and designed to pull you back in. That constant access is exactly what makes modern platforms more addictive, and more dangerous for young people, than the casino floor ever was.

The rise of mobile and sports betting, engagement-driven platform design, and the blurring line between gaming and gambling all raise public health and policy questions that affect young people first.

Research

How digital sports betting reaches teens

Accepted to the Oxford Journal of Student Scholarship

Since the Supreme Court overturned the federal ban on sports betting in 2018, legal wagering in the U.S. has grown rapidly, and most bets are now placed online. Adolescents develop gambling problems at higher rates than adults, so this shift hits them hardest. The paper argues that four mechanisms work together as a single, reinforcing system rather than as separate risks:

01

Smartphone accessibility

Removes the friction that used to prevent impulsive betting and embeds wagering into daily routines.

02

Gaming–gambling convergence

Gambling-like mechanics in games get young people used to paying for chance-based rewards years before the legal age.

03

Manipulative app design

Betting platform interfaces are deliberately built to make losing money easy.

04

Social media normalization

Advertising and gambling livestreams make betting look like an ordinary part of sports and online culture.

Current safeguards like age verification and voluntary spending limits have largely failed. The paper proposes stronger measures, including biometric identity verification at registration and mandatory restrictions on gambling content across streaming platforms.
